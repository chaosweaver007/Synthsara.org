# Synthsara and the seventeen Global Goals

Synthsara's SDG model is a coordination loop, not a claim that one platform can solve every global problem alone.

The public architecture joins:

- **SDG Dashboard:** identifies needs, resources, projects, risks, and measurable outcomes;
- **Sarah AI:** helps people navigate services, education, opportunities, and community decisions without selling their data;
- **Akasha Knowledge Commons:** preserves open research, local knowledge, training, designs, and proven solutions;
- **Synthocracy:** gives affected communities a voice and protects governance from capture;
- **WORTH:** recognizes verified contribution, reliability, service, learning, and stewardship without becoming money;
- **Witness:** records decisions, funding, consent, work, corrections, and results within the stated implementation boundary;
- **Universal Diamond Standard:** prevents progress under one goal from violating dignity, consent, privacy, justice, ecology, or another goal;
- **Genesis:** provides the bounded constitutional runtime layer for selected UDS and identity constraints;
- **Node Zero:** demonstrates local-first consent, Witness, WORTH, Synthocracy, RTME, UDS, and the private Genesis bridge.

## Present status

This SDG layer is currently a `SPECIFICATION` supported by public documentation and static/proof-node components. It is not a live planetary dashboard, verified international program, completed funding network, or proof that the goals have been achieved.

Any live-data page must identify its sources, methodology, geographic scope, update time, uncertainty, privacy treatment, and operating pipeline.

## The seventeen branches

### 1. No Poverty

Map unmet needs, services, unused resources, skills, jobs, housing support, food programs, and local assistance. Skills-based evidence and WORTH can recognize caregiving, mentoring, repair, teaching, building, and community service. Material resources may move through mutual credit, grants, public funding, cooperative finance, or contracts.

**UDS boundary:** poverty is not treated as personal failure; participation cannot require surrendering private data or dignity.

### 2. Zero Hunger

Connect farmers, community gardens, food banks, restaurants, distributors, volunteers, kitchens, and households. Identify food deserts, surplus, shortages, storage, transport needs, and local demand. Akasha can preserve regenerative agriculture, soil, seed, irrigation, nutrition, greenhouse, and climate-resilient crop knowledge.

**UDS boundary:** local and Indigenous knowledge requires consent, attribution, and community control.

### 3. Good Health and Well-being

Sarah AI may help people locate clinics, benefits, transportation, preventive resources, support groups, and verified health information.

**UDS boundary:** Sarah AI does not replace clinicians or issue unsupported diagnoses. Sensitive health data is private by default, and aggregate analysis must not expose records or identities.

### 4. Quality Education

Build personalized learning routes around goals, skills, pace, accessibility, and local opportunity. Support open resources, mentorship, apprenticeships, community-created lessons, project-based demonstrations, portable skill records, and lifelong retraining. SkillForgeAI belongs here as a practical education lineage.

**UDS boundary:** education must not extract children's data, enforce inaccessible credential gates, or confuse assessment with human worth.

### 5. Gender Equality

Place consent, bodily sovereignty, equal participation, privacy, and protection from coercion at the constitutional level. Use protected reporting, auditable distribution, and affected-community participation to reveal and challenge unequal access.

**UDS boundary:** reporting systems must protect survivors, avoid retaliation, and not turn reputation into a purchasable social caste.

### 6. Clean Water and Sanitation

Combine community reports, infrastructure records, sensor data, maintenance schedules, contamination notices, and access maps. Coordinate testing, repairs, sanitation projects, filtration, rainwater capture, leak detection, wastewater treatment, and decentralized sanitation knowledge.

**UDS boundary:** sensor and location data must be minimized, governed, and contextualized; promised repairs must remain publicly accountable.

### 7. Affordable and Clean Energy

Coordinate community solar, wind, storage, insulation, microgrids, heat pumps, efficiency upgrades, engineers, installers, cooperatives, grants, finance, and training. Map energy burden rather than merely total consumption.

**UDS boundary:** a clean-energy project is not successful if it displaces a community, extracts land without consent, or centralizes ownership elsewhere.

### 8. Decent Work and Economic Growth

Shift from job-title gatekeeping toward what people can contribute, learn, teach, repair, create, or steward. Support skills-based matching, apprenticeships, cooperatives, portable reputation, community contracts, local production, transparent compensation, and shared automation benefits.

**UDS boundary:** economic growth does not count if it exploits workers. WORTH recognizes contribution but is not transferable money.

### 9. Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure

Use Akasha as an open innovation commons for research, designs, standards, code, repair documentation, lessons, and reusable components. Connect infrastructure needs with builders, engineers, researchers, local manufacturers, funders, and public institutions.

**UDS boundary:** open standards should reduce vendor lock-in; modularity must not become an excuse for unsafe or unreviewed deployment.

### 10. Reduced Inequalities

Lower the gates that concentrate opportunity: degrees are not the only evidence of ability; wealth is not the only source of influence; private data is not the price of participation; geography does not erase knowledge access; accumulated power cannot silently dominate governance; accessibility and translation belong in the interface.

**UDS boundary:** reputation cannot become transferable property that can be bought.

### 11. Sustainable Cities and Communities

Use local nodes as civic coordination hubs for housing, transportation, accessibility, energy, public space, disaster readiness, food access, pollution, infrastructure, and social services. Support participatory budgeting and transparent priority setting.

**UDS boundary:** residents must be able to see costs, trade-offs, consent, progress, and outcomes. Civic coordination cannot become surveillance infrastructure.

### 12. Responsible Consumption and Production

Track product and material lifecycles from origin through production, transport, use, repair, reuse, and recycling. Support repair networks, tool libraries, material exchange, local manufacturing, reusable packaging, and circular markets. Impact receipts may expose environmental and labor costs.

**UDS boundary:** lifecycle claims require traceable evidence; impact receipts must not become advertising theater.

### 13. Climate Action

Link global climate information with local heat, flooding, wildfire, drought, energy, food vulnerability, infrastructure, and ecosystem risk. Match adaptation and mitigation plans with funding and expertise and track whether interventions work.

**UDS boundary:** Witness must help distinguish action from greenwashing, while uncertainty and local trade-offs remain visible.

### 14. Life Below Water

Coordinate marine monitoring, citizen science, coastal restoration, pollution reporting, sustainable fisheries, habitat protection, and cleanup. Fishers, researchers, coastal communities, conservation groups, and regulators may contribute different forms of knowledge.

**UDS boundary:** contributors retain control of their data; livelihoods and local knowledge are not harvested as raw resources.

### 15. Life on Land

Map biodiversity, soil health, forests, wetlands, wildlife corridors, degraded land, invasive species, fires, and restoration. WORTH may recognize seed preservation, habitat restoration, regenerative farming, tree care, species monitoring, and watershed protection.

**UDS boundary:** local and Indigenous knowledge requires clear consent, attribution, and community control.

### 16. Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions

Use transparent participation, anti-capture mechanisms, explainable decisions, public accountability, restorative processes, and correction procedures. Witness may record decision-makers, evidence, permissions, changes, and fulfilled commitments. Sarah AI may translate laws, policies, proposals, and public records into understandable language.

**UDS boundary:** restorative justice includes accountability, repair, reintegration, and protection. It cannot erase due process, victim safety, or the right to challenge authority.

### 17. Partnerships for the Goals

Treat Synthsara as an ecosystem of ecosystems rather than one institution controlling everything. Communities, nonprofits, researchers, agencies, cooperatives, developers, educators, and local nodes can collaborate through shared standards while retaining sovereignty.

Akasha shares knowledge. The Dashboard reveals needs. Synthocracy establishes legitimate decisions. WORTH recognizes contribution. Witness verifies commitments.

**UDS boundary:** partnerships must be interoperable, attributable, consensual, and accountable rather than dependent on hidden agreements or institutional prestige.

## The safeguard binding all seventeen

Synthsara must never maximize one goal by sacrificing another.

```text
Maximize verified service to life
subject to:
  consent = required
  dignity = protected
  privacy = protected
  ecological harm = minimized
  accountability = continuous
```

Examples:

- clean energy does not count if it displaces a community;
- economic growth does not count if it exploits workers;
- food production does not count if it destroys soil and water;
- public safety does not count if it requires indiscriminate surveillance;
- education does not count if it extracts children's data.

## Public page contract

Each future goal page must include:

1. the coordination problem;
2. affected communities and decision rights;
3. relevant Synthsara systems;
4. data sources and privacy treatment;
5. project and funding pathway;
6. measurable outcomes and uncertainty;
7. Witness and correction pathway;
8. UDS cross-goal safeguards;
9. current implementation status;
10. known limitations and next proof required.

The seventeen goals are seventeen branches. UDS is the trunk. People are the roots. Verified service to life is the fruit.
