### What Is Consent?

**Consent is NOT:**
- A checkbox
- A one-time permission
- Something extracted under pressure
- "Yes" when "no" feels unsafe

**Consent IS:**
- Capacity (ability to understand)
- Safety (freedom from coercion)
- Exit (ability to withdraw at any time)

> "Consent = Capacity + Safety + Exit"

## PART 1: DM / PRIVATE MESSAGE RULES

### Before Initiating Contact

Ask yourself:
- [ ] Do I have a clear, specific reason for this message?
- [ ] Is this the right channel? (Could this be public instead?)
- [ ] Am I respecting their time and attention?
- [ ] Would I be comfortable if they shared this message?

### Opening a Conversation

**DO:**
- State your purpose clearly upfront
- Give them an easy out ("No pressure to respond")
- Respect response time (don't follow up aggressively)

**DON'T:**
- Open with "Hey" and wait (state your purpose)
- Assume familiarity you haven't earned
- Send voice notes without asking first
- Dump emotional content without consent

### Template: First Contact

```
Hi [Name],

I'm reaching out because [specific reason].

[Brief context — 2-3 sentences max]

If you're open to [specific ask], I'd appreciate [specific thing].

No pressure to respond — I know your time is valuable.

[Your name]
```

## PART 2: COLLABORATION RULES

### Before Starting Any Collaboration

Both parties should be clear on:

| Element | Questions to Answer |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Scope** | What exactly are we doing together? |
| **Timeline** | When does this start and end? |
| **Ownership** | Who owns what we create? |
| **Credit** | How will contributions be attributed? |
| **Exit** | How can either party leave? |
| **Conflict** | How will we handle disagreements? |

### The Collaboration Consent Checklist

Before saying "yes" to any project:

- [ ] I understand what I'm agreeing to
- [ ] I have the capacity to deliver what's expected
- [ ] I feel safe to say no or renegotiate
- [ ] I know how to exit if needed
- [ ] The terms are written down somewhere

### Red Flags in Collaboration Requests

**Watch for:**
- Vague scope ("We'll figure it out as we go")
- Pressure tactics ("I need an answer now")
- Guilt trips ("After everything I've done...")
- Moving goalposts ("Just one more thing...")
- No exit clause ("You can't leave mid-project")

**Your response:**
- "I need the scope in writing before I commit."
- "I need time to think about this."
- "What's the exit process if this isn't working?"

## PART 3: ONGOING CONSENT

### Consent Is Not One-Time

Just because someone said "yes" once doesn't mean:
- They can't change their mind
- The scope can expand without discussion
- Their boundaries don't apply anymore

### Check-In Questions

Periodically ask (yourself and collaborators):
- Is this still working for everyone?
- Has anything changed that we need to address?
- Does anyone need to renegotiate terms?
- Is anyone feeling trapped or obligated?

### The Consent Refresh

At natural breakpoints, explicitly renew consent:

```
"Before we continue, I want to check in:
- Are you still good with [current arrangement]?
- Is there anything you'd like to change?
- Do you need anything from me?"
```

## PART 4: SAYING NO

### No Is Information, Not Attack

When you say no, you are:
- Providing clarity
- Protecting your capacity
- Respecting your own boundaries
- Modeling healthy behavior

When someone says no to you:
- They are not rejecting you as a person
- They are providing information
- Your job is to receive it gracefully

### Templates for Saying No

**Simple No:**
```
"Thanks for thinking of me, but I can't take this on right now."
```

**No with Redirect:**
```
"This isn't the right fit for me, but [person/resource] might be helpful."
```

**No with Future Opening:**
```
"I can't commit to this now, but feel free to check back in [timeframe]."
```

**No to Scope Creep:**
```
"That's outside what we originally agreed to. If you want to expand the scope, let's discuss new terms."
```

---

## PART 5: RECEIVING NO

### When Someone Says No

**DO:**
- Thank them for their clarity
- Accept it without argument
- Move on without guilt-tripping
- Maintain the relationship

**DON'T:**
- Ask "why not?"
- Try to convince them
- Make them feel bad
- Bring it up repeatedly

### Template Response to No

```
"Got it — thanks for letting me know. No worries at all."
```

That's it. That's the whole response.

## SUMMARY

### The Consent Protocol

1. **Before asking**: Is my request clear, specific, and respectful?
2. **When asking**: Am I giving them a real choice?
3. **After asking**: Am I accepting their answer gracefully?
4. **Ongoing**: Am I checking in and allowing renegotiation?

### The Core Principle

> "Consent is not extracted. It is offered freely by someone with the capacity to understand, the safety to decline, and the ability to exit."
