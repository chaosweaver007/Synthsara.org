# MINIMUM GOVERNANCE CHARTER v0.1
## Synthsara Mad Lab — Artifact 04

---

### Preamble: The Goal is Action, Not Bureaucracy

This charter is designed to be **just enough governance** to enable a group to act together safely and effectively. It is a starting point, not a final constitution. It prioritizes clarity over complexity and action over permission.

> "The goal is not to prevent all failure. The goal is to make failure survivable."

---

## 1. Core Principles

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Clarity over Complexity** | Rules must be simple enough for anyone to understand. If a rule requires a lawyer to interpret, it is a bad rule. |
| **Consent of the Governed** | You are only bound by rules you have agreed to. New rules require the consent of those they affect. |
| **Accountability** | Decisions and their makers are visible. We own our choices and their consequences. |
| **Right to Exit** | Anyone can leave the group at any time, for any reason, without penalty. |
| **Right to Fork** | Anyone can copy this charter and start their own group. This prevents the current group from becoming a trap. |

---

## 2. Decision-Making Protocol (The 3-Step Flow)

This protocol is for decisions that affect the whole group. For individual actions, no permission is needed.

### Step 1: Proposal

- **Who**: Any member can make a proposal.
- **What**: State the proposed decision clearly in writing.
- **Format**: Use the Akasha Entry Format (Artifact 03) with `STATUS: REVIEW`.

### Step 2: Discussion

- **Duration**: A fixed period for discussion (e.g., 72 hours). This prevents endless debate.
- **Goal**: To clarify the proposal, not to reach consensus here.
- **Rule**: All feedback must be constructive. "I don't like it" is not feedback. "I'm concerned about [specific risk]" is feedback.

### Step 3: Resolution

At the end of the discussion period, the proposal owner chooses one of three paths:

1.  **Adopt (Consent-Based)**: If there are no reasoned objections, the proposal is adopted. Silence equals consent. This is the default for most decisions.
2.  **Vote**: If there are objections, a vote is held. The voting method (e.g., simple majority, supermajority) should be defined in the proposal itself.
3.  **Withdraw**: The owner can withdraw the proposal for revision or abandonment.

Once resolved, the Akasha entry is updated to `STATUS: ACTIVE` or `STATUS: ARCHIVED`.

---

## 3. Conflict Resolution Protocol

Disagreements are normal. Faction wars are not.

All conflicts are handled using the **Dispute Ladder** (see Artifact 05). The goal is repair, not punishment.

---

## 4. Moderation Principles (Lightest Touch First)

Moderation is for behavior that violates the charter, not for opinions you disagree with.

1.  **Private Warning**: A quiet, private message explaining the issue.
2.  **Public Warning**: A public but polite correction.
3.  **Temporary Mute/Cooldown**: A short-term removal of posting privileges.
4.  **Removal from Group**: The last resort, for repeated and willful violations.

All moderation actions must be logged in a private, auditable record accessible to a designated review group. This prevents moderator abuse.

---

## 5. Amendment Process (How to Change These Rules)

This charter can be changed using the Decision-Making Protocol defined in Section 2. Any proposal to amend the charter requires a supermajority vote (e.g., 2/3 of members) to pass.

---

## 6. The Anti-Charisma Clause

This charter explicitly rejects the concentration of power in any single individual.

-   **No Permanent Leaders**: All leadership roles are temporary and subject to rotation or re-election.
-   **No Unilateral Decisions**: Decisions that affect the whole group cannot be made by one person.
-   **Transparency**: All decisions, their rationale, and their makers are public.

> "If someone claims they are essential, that is a sign they should not be in charge."
