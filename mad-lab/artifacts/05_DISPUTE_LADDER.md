# DISPUTE LADDER
## Synthsara Mad Lab — Artifact 05

---

### Preamble: Conflict is Information

Disputes are not failures; they are data. They reveal where expectations are misaligned, where boundaries are unclear, or where systems are weak. This ladder provides a structured process for turning conflict into clarity.

> "Slow is smooth. Smooth is fast."

---

## The Ladder: A Step-by-Step Process

Start at the lowest possible rung. Only escalate if the current step fails.

### Rung 1: Self-Reflection (The 24-Hour Pause)

**Action**: Before engaging, stop. Do not react. Wait 24 hours.

**Ask Yourself**:
- What is my role in this situation?
- Am I assuming intent? Could there be another explanation?
- Is this a pattern or a single event?
- What is the most generous interpretation of the other person's actions?
- What outcome do I actually want?

**Goal**: To move from reaction to intention. Often, this step resolves the issue without any external action.

---

### Rung 2: Direct Communication (One-to-One)

**Action**: Reach out directly and privately to the other person involved.

**Use "I" statements**:
- **Instead of**: "You were disrespectful."
- **Try**: "I felt disrespected when..."

**Structure the Conversation**:
1.  **State your observation**: "When you said [X]..."
2.  **State your interpretation/feeling**: "...I felt [Y]."
3.  **Ask for their perspective**: "Can you help me understand your point of view?"
4.  **Make a clear request**: "In the future, I would appreciate it if you would [Z]."

**Goal**: To resolve the issue with the minimum number of people involved.

---

### Rung 3: Mediation (Bring in a Neutral Third Party)

**Action**: If direct communication fails or feels unsafe, ask a neutral, trusted third party (a Mediator) to facilitate a conversation.

**The Mediator's Role**:
- To ensure both parties feel heard.
- To enforce the communication rules (no personal attacks, use "I" statements).
- To guide the conversation toward a mutually agreeable outcome.
- **NOT** to take sides or declare a winner.

**Process**:
1.  Mediator speaks to each party separately to understand their perspective.
2.  Mediator brings both parties together for a structured conversation.
3.  Mediator helps them co-create a solution or agreement.

**Goal**: To de-escalate and find a path forward with support.

---

### Rung 4: Group Review (Formal Accountability)

**Action**: If mediation fails, bring the issue to a pre-designated review group (e.g., a council of stewards or auditors).

**This is for serious issues**: Repeated charter violations, harassment, or bad-faith actions.

**Process**:
1.  A formal, written complaint is submitted.
2.  All parties involved are given the opportunity to provide their perspective in writing.
3.  The review group investigates, focusing on actions and evidence, not hearsay.
4.  The review group makes a decision based on the charter's principles and moderation policies.

**Goal**: To provide a formal, fair process for accountability when lower rungs fail.

---

### Rung 5: Exit (The Final Step)

**Action**: If you have gone through the ladder and still feel the situation is untenable, you always have the right to leave.

**This is not failure**. It is a valid choice to protect your own well-being when a group's culture is no longer a fit.

---

## Principles of Healthy Conflict

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Attack the Problem, Not the Person** | Focus on the issue, not on character or identity. |
| **Assume Good Faith (Until Proven Otherwise)** | Start from the assumption that the other person is not trying to cause harm. |
| **Listen to Understand, Not to Respond** | Hear their perspective fully before formulating your own response. |
| **Seek Repair, Not Punishment** | The goal is to fix the process and restore trust, not to make someone pay. |
| **Log the Lesson, Not the Blame** | After a dispute is resolved, document the lesson learned for the whole group in the Akasha, without naming individuals. |
